## PHASE 2 — Module 1: Crazy Chords Foundation

### Lesson 1 — Music Notes & Names

**Music Notes**

There are 7 basic (natural) notes in the music language, using the first 7 letters of the English alphabet:

**A – B – C – D – E – F – G**

The note names repeat continuously:

`A - B - C - D - E - F - G - A - B - C - D - E - F - G ...`

On a keyboard, these are the **white keys**.

The **C Major Scale** is one of the first scales most musicians learn. It uses all the basic notes, in this order:

`C - D - E - F - G - A - B - C`

**Sharps & Flats**

- **Sharp (♯):** raise the note you are playing to the note directly *above* it.
- **Flat (♭):** lower the note you are playing to the note directly *below* it.

Sharps and flats use the basic/natural note names as their foundation (e.g., C♯ or E♭).

On each **black key**, there are two note names, because each black key is simultaneously the sharp of one note and the flat of another (e.g., the key between C and D is both C♯ and D♭).

### Lesson 2 — Intervals & The Number System

**Intervals**

An interval = the distance between two notes.

**The Number System** assigns a number to each note of the major scale (7 notes). In the key of C:

`C(1) - D(2) - E(3) - F(4) - G(5) - A(6) - B(7) - C(8)`

This is "musical slang" — a note's name can be substituted for its number (e.g., E = the "3", A = the "6").

**Basic Intervals (from C):**

| Interval | Notes |
|---|---|
| 2nd | C(1) – D(2) |
| 3rd | C(1) – E(3) |
| 4th | C(1) – F(4) |
| 5th | C(1) – G(5) |
| 6th | C(1) – A(6) |
| 7th | C(1) – B(7) |
| 8th / Octave | C(1) – C(8) |

**Extended Intervals**

Continuing the number system past the octave:

`C(8) - D(9) - E(10) - F(11) - G(12) - A(13) - B(14) - C(15)`

| Interval | Notes |
|---|---|
| 9th | C(1) – D(9) |
| 10th | C(1) – E(10) |
| 11th | C(1) – F(11) |
| 12th | C(1) – G(12) |
| 13th | C(1) – A(13) |
| 14th | C(1) – B(14) |
| 15th / Double Octave | C(1) – C(15) |

**Three Special Intervals**

- **Half (H) Step:** move one note to the immediate right or left (e.g., C to C♯, or C to B).
- **Whole (W) Step:** two half steps in either direction.
- **Minor (min) 3rd:** three half steps in either direction.

### The Major Scale Formula

Understanding *how* the major scale is constructed (via intervals and the number system) — not just memorizing notes — is the foundation for building any chord.

**Formula (Whole/Half step pattern):**

`W - W - H - W - W - W - H`

Applied to C Major:

`1(C) - W - 2(D) - W - 3(E) - H - 4(F) - W - 5(G) - W - 6(A) - W - 7(B) - H - 1(C)`

**Major Scales & Fingerings (all keys)**

| Key | Notes | RH Fingering | LH Fingering |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C | 1 2 3 1 2 3 4 5 | 5 4 3 2 1 3 2 1 |

| D | D-E-F♯-G-A-B-C♯-D | 1 2 3 1 2 3 4 5 | 5 4 3 2 1 3 2 1 |

### Lesson 5 — How To Build Chords

**Basic Chords** are divided into two categories: **Triads** (3-note chords) and **4-note chords**.

- Triads are formed using **1 – 3 – 5** of the major scale (two exceptions apply — see suspended chords below).
- 4-note chords add the 6th or 7th note of the major scale to the triad (e.g., 1-3-5-6 or 1-3-5-7).
- Any note in a triad or 4-note chord can be sharped or flatted **except the root (1)**.

**Triads**

| Chord | Formula | Example (C) | Symbol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major Triad | 1 - 3 - 5 | C - E - G | maj |
| Minor Triad | 1 - ♭3 - 5 | C - E♭ - G | min |
| Diminished Triad | 1 - ♭3 - ♭5 | C - E♭ - G♭ | dim |
| Augmented Triad | 1 - 3 - ♯5 | C - E - G♯ | aug |
| Suspended 4th Triad | 1 - 4 - 5 | C - F - G | sus4 |
| Suspended 2nd Triad | 1 - 2 - 5 | C - D - G | sus2 |

**7th / 6th Chords**

| Chord | Formula | Example (C) | Symbol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major 6th | 1 - 3 - 5 - 6 | C - E - G - A | Maj6 |
| Minor 6th | 1 - ♭3 - 5 - 6 | C - E♭ - G - A | Min6 |
| Major 7th | 1 - 3 - 5 - 7 | C - E - G - B | maj7 |
| Minor 7th | 1 - ♭3 - 5 - ♭7 | C - E♭ - G - B♭ | min7 |
| Minor 7th (♭5) | 1 - ♭3 - ♭5 - ♭7 | C - E♭ - G♭ - B♭ | min7♭5 / -7♭5 |
| Diminished 7th | 1 - ♭3 - ♭5 - ♭♭7 | C - E♭ - G♭ - A | dim7 |
| Dominant 7th Suspended | 1 - 4 - 5 - ♭7 | C - F - G - B♭ | 7sus4 |
| Diminished Major 7th | 1 - ♭3 - ♭5 - 7 | C - E♭ - G♭ - B | dim Maj7 |
| Minor Major 7th | 1 - ♭3 - 5 - 7 | C - E♭ - G - B | min+Maj7 |
| Dominant 7th | 1 - 3 - 5 - ♭7 | C - E - G - B♭ | dom7 / 7 |

*Note: double flats (♭♭) and double sharps (♯♯) are the "whole step" versions of the half-step flat/sharp.*

**Homework:** Play all chords above in every key, in both hands, naming each chord aloud as you play it (e.g., "A♭ Maj7," "C min7"). Goal: confident, hesitation-free recall.

### Lesson 6 — Diatonic Chords

Every note in the major scale has a specific chord built on it — these are the **Diatonic Chords**. Most songs default to these chords in one form or another.

**Diatonic Chords of C Major (Roman Numeral System)**

| Degree | Chord | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| I | C maj7 | C - E - G - B |
| II | D min7 | D - F - A - C |
| III | E min7 | E - G - B - D |
| IV | F Maj7 | F - A - C - E |
| V | G dom7 | G - B - D - F |
| VI | A min7 | A - C - E - G |
| VII | B min7(♭5) | B - D - F - A |

**Homework:** Play diatonic chords in every key, both hands, naming each Roman numeral (I maj7, V dom7, etc.) as you play, to build on-the-fly recall for performance.

### Adding Tensions Worksheet (v3.0)

**Rules for Tensions**

- Tensions are only applied when the **7th** of a chord is present.
- Tension choices: **9th, 11th, 13th.**
- Tensions can be raised (♯) or lowered (♭) a half step — **exception:** the 11th cannot be flatted (it becomes a 3rd).
- Multiple tensions can combine, e.g., `C maj7(9, ♯11)`.
- Tensions are "icing on the cake" — don't overuse.

**Available Tensions by Chord Type**

| Chord (formula) | Available Tensions |
|---|---|
| Major 7th (1-3-5-7) | 9, ♯11, 13 |
| Minor 7th (1-♭3-5-♭7) | 9, 11, 13 |
| Dominant 7th (1-3-5-♭7) | ♭9, 9, ♯9, ♮11, ♯11, ♭13, 13 |
| Minor 7th (♭5) (1-♭3-♭5-♭7) | 9, 11 |
| Diminished 7th (1-♭3-♭5-♭♭7) | ♭9, 9, 11, ♭13 |
| Dominant 7th Sus4 (1-4-5-♭7) | 9, 13 |
| Diminished Major 7th (1-♭3-♭5-7) | 9 |
| Minor Major 7th (1-♭3-5-7) | 9 |
| Augmented Maj7 (1-3-♯5-7) | 9, ♯11 |

**Recommended Chords & Tension Combinations**

*Reminder: any chord chosen must share the melody note ("Shared Notes Principle").*

### Lesson 8 — Common Gospel Progressions

*Practice tip: use "Play-Along Backing Tracks" as your drilling tool.*

**Key Points**

- These chord progressions are the building blocks for almost all gospel songs — practice them.
- Diminished 7th chords (1-♭3-♭5-♭♭7) are, 9 times out of 10, built on notes *outside* the major scale of the key you're in. They function as **connecting chords** (covered in more depth in Module 2).
- Basic diatonic versions of these progressions are just a starting point — substitute in Advanced Gospel Chords (Lesson 7) to create new ideas.

### Left Hand Chord Voicings & Low Interval Limits

**Low Interval Limits (LILs) Explained**

The goal for left-hand voicings is to play as *low* as possible on the piano without sounding muddy. LILs tell you the lowest note from which a given interval still sounds clean.

| Interval | Lowest Note |
|---|---|
| Min 2nd (♭2) | C♯3 |
| Maj 2nd | C3 |
| Min 3rd (♭3) | A3 |
| Maj 3rd | A♭2 |
| Perfect 4th | G2 |
| Min 5th (♭5) / Tritone | C3 |
| Perfect 5th | E1 |
| Min 6th (♭6) | C2 |
| Perfect 6th | D2 |
| Min 7th (♭7) | D♭2 |
| Maj 7th | E♭2 |
| Octave | Infinite |

You *can* stack intervals — e.g., Perfect 5th + Minor 3rd = 1-5-♭7.

**Recommended Left Hand Chord Voicings**

| Voicing | Use for |
|---|---|
| 1 - ♭7 | Dominant, Dom7(sus4), Minor 7th chords |
| 1 - 5 - ♭7 | Minor 7th, Dom7(sus4), Dominant chords |
| 1 - 5 - 7 | Major chords |
| 1 - 5 - 9 | Major, Minor, Dominant 7th, Dom7(sus4) |
| 1 - 5 - 9 - 3 | Major chords |
| 1 - 5 - ♭3 | Minor chords |
| 1 - 5 - 3 | Major chords |
| 1 - ♭6 | Augmented, Major chord 1st inversion (1/3) |
| 1 - 6 | Major (1/5 inversion), Minor 6th, Diminished 7th |
| 1 - 6 - ♭3 | Diminished chords |
| 1 - 3 - ♭7 (or 1-♭7-3) | Dominant chords |

*You can add further notes to these LH voicings based on available tensions for the chord being played.*

## PHASE 4

### Chord Stack System

The **Chord Stack System** organizes all chords and available tensions into five categories, by increasing complexity, corresponding to gospel-music styles:

1. **Traditional** — Major triads, Minor triads, Augmented triads, Minor 6th chords, Dominant 7th chords (no tensions)
2. **Worship / CCM** — Major triad (add2), Minor triad (add2)
3. **Churchy** — Minor 7th, Dominant ♭9, 9 & ♯9 chords, diminished chords, diatonic chords
4. **Early Contemporary** — Major7 (9,13), Minor7 (9,11), Dom7sus4 (9,13)
5. **Late Contemporary** — All chords with all tension possibilities (see Adding Tensions worksheet)

**Note:** each category *includes* the chords from all prior categories (e.g., Churchy includes Traditional + Worship/CCM + Churchy).

**Chord Stack Combos (CSCs):** You can combine categories within a single song — e.g., play Churchy stacks, insert a chord from Modern Contemporary, then return to Churchy. This is how new progressions, approaches, and reharmonizations are created.

---

### Human Emotional Grid

A mapping of harmonic "weight" to emotional arc, on a 0–50+ scale. *(Uses "1 to 1" logic — happy emotion = major chords — but more nuanced emotional tapestries can be built by combining elements in different orders.)*

| Score Range | Music Elements | Emotional Stage | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–15 | Minor / Diminished chords, Minor scales w/ min6 | Weakest Negative | Powerlessness, sadness, depression |
| 16–25 | Dominant chords, Minor 7th chords, Blues scale, Minor scales w/ maj6 | Power Assertion | Regaining self-respect, revenge, defeating the devil |
| 26–39 | Major 7th / Minor 7th chords, arpeggios, scales | Hopeful / Reflect | Hope, faith, last bit of strength |
| 40–50 | (transition zone) | Optimistic | Light at the end of the tunnel; relying on God — "if it happened for them, it can happen for me" |
| 40–50+ | Major triads, Major scales & arpeggios | Joy / Exuberation | "I made it!" — victorious jubilation |

## PHASE 5

### How To Reharm Songs — The Shared Notes Principle

**"The Holy Grail of Crazy Gospel Chords"**

**Shared Notes Principle:** *the art of borrowing notes & chords from other keys to use in service of the melody in a song.*

**Key Rules**

- Any chord borrowed from another key **must contain the melody note** — either as a basic chord tone (1-3-5-7) or as an available tension (9-11-13), including flatted/sharped versions.
- Review Chords, tensions, and Common Progressions (Module 1, Lessons 6–8) before attempting this.
- Always keep the melody note as the **top note** of every chord to start — this speeds up applying the Shared Notes Principle.

*Next lesson covers not just* what *to play, but* how *to play it for full effect.*

### How To Use Modes & Modal Interchange

Understanding modes requires understanding: the Number System, Diatonic Chords, and the Major Scale.

If you're in the key of C, playing "in a mode" simply means playing the C major scale starting on a different scale degree.

**The Seven Modes**

| Mode | Starts on | Number Formula | Notes (from C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ionian | 1st note | 1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8 | C D E F G A B C |
| Dorian | 2nd note | 1-2-♭3-4-5-6-♭7-8 | D E F G A B C D |
| Phrygian | 3rd note | 1-♭2-♭3-4-5-♭6-♭7-8 | E F G A B C D E |
| Lydian | 4th note | 1-2-3-♯4-5-6-7-8 | F G A B C D E F |
| Mixolydian | 5th note | 1-2-3-4-5-6-♭7-8 | G A B C D E F G |
| Aeolian | 6th note | 1-2-♭3-4-5-♭6-♭7-8 | A B C D E F G A |
| Locrian | 7th note | 1-♭2-♭3-4-♭5-♭6-♭7-8 | B C D E F G A B |

**Diatonic Chords (for reference, C Major)**

| Chord | Notes | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| C (I maj7) | C - E - G - B | Ionian |
| D (II min7) | D - F - A - C | Dorian |
| E (III min7) | E - G - B - D | Phrygian |
| F (IV Maj7) | F - A - C - E | Lydian |
| G (V dom7) | G - B - D - F | Mixolydian |
| A (VI min7) | A - C - E - G | Aeolian |
| B (VII min7♭5) | B - D - F - A | Locrian |

## PHASE 6

### Common Passing Chord Bass Movements

Certain bass movements recur constantly in gospel music — the **chord types/stacks used over them can vary, but the bass movement stays constant**. Any of the movements below can be used with **any** Chord Stack category, as long as you honor the **MCR formula** and always consider the song's melody.

**List of Common Passing Chord Bass Movements**

1. ♭5 – 4 – 3
2. ♭5 – 7 – 3
3. ♭7 – 6 – ♭6 – 5
4. 5 – 1 – 4 *(or 5 – ♭5 – 4, also the sub-dominant version)*
5. 6 – 2 – 5 – 1 – 4
6. 3 – ♭3 – 2
7. 4 – 3 – 2 / 1 – 7 – 6 / ♭7 – 6 – 5 *(usually used approaching minor chords — but be creative; if it sounds good, it's fine)*

> *Remember: you can use ANY Chord Stack category over these bass movements, as long as you honor the MCR formula and the melody of the song you're playing.*

---

### Chord Stack Passing Movements

Similar principle to the above: bass movements recur regardless of the specific chord stack used over them. All movements below work with any Chord Stack category — honor the **MCR formula** and always factor in the melody.

**Churchy Chord Stacks**
- 1 - 2 - ♯2 - 3
- 1 - ♭2 - 2 - ♭3 - 3
- 1 - ♭7 - 6 - ♭6 - 5
- 4 - ♯4 - 5

**Inversions Climb**
- I major → II minor → VII major inversions, over secondary dominants and ii-V-i's / VII-III-VI's, etc.

**Contemporary Chord Stacks (Micro Resolutions)**
- Suspended chords → Dominant (♭9) → Minor chords

**Worship / CCM Maj add2 Chords**
- 6 - 7 - 1
- 7 - 1 - 3 - 4/5
- 6 - 7 - ♭2 - 2

**Classical / Traditional Chord Stack Walkdowns**
- ♭6 - ♭5 - 3 - 6
- 1 - 7 - 6
- I maj - II min6 - I/3
- I major - IV min6 - I/3
- I major - II Major triad/1 - IV min6/1 - 1
